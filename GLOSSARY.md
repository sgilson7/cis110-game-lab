# Glossary

The source of truth for what each word on the assignment page means. Strings, tests, the plan and the README use a term in the sense given here. The page shows a definition when a reader hovers the term.

Write each entry as a `### Term` heading, then one or two plain sentences, then an optional `Not:` line listing words that must not be used for it. A number a rule decides is a `{placeholder}` the core fills, so the glossary cannot disagree with the rules. Keep entries in alphabetical order. `crates/core/tests/copy.rs` checks the format, the placeholders and the `Not:` words; `TONE.md` governs the prose. The `Not:` lines also keep Builder's internal words off the student page (GL-4).

### Change request

Something your team wants changed in the prototype, written in your own words as one row of the Changes sheet.

Not: triage, ticket, bug report

### Checkpoint

A date when part of the project should be in your shared folder. Each checkpoint is due at {due_time}.

Not: milestone, stage, sprint

### Design document

The Word file that describes your game or tool in your team's own words, under the template's ten headings.

Not: brief, spec, specification, GDD

### Prototype

A first working version of your game or tool, built from your folder as it stands on {long:C5}. It is ungraded.

Not: alpha, beta, MVP

### Shared folder

The one OneDrive folder that holds your team's three files and is shared with every member and the instructor.

Not: repository, repo, project directory

### Team

The {team_min} to {team_max} classmates who design one game or tool together.

Not: squad, crew

### Team discussion

Your team's own discussion in Blackboard, where you introduce yourselves and pitch ideas before your shared folder exists. Only your team and the instructor can read it.

Not: chat, group chat, forum
