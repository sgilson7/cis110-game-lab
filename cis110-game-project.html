Collaborative Game Design Project

In this project, you work with a team of 3 to 5 classmates to design a small video game. You don't write any code. Your team describes the game in three Microsoft Office files. One of you also records a short video presenting your slides. I then turn your design into a game you can play in a web browser, using a game-making framework I built for my PhD.

Would your team rather design a tool than a game? Use the tool version of this page, linked below. It has the same dates and grading.

The project runs alongside the SAM modules. You write the design document after the Word modules and fill in the game data while you learn Excel. The slides come before the PowerPoint modules, and they need only basic slides and shapes.

All deadlines are at 5:00 PM ET. Late work loses 20%.

Deliverables

What to submit

Your team keeps three files in one shared OneDrive folder. Download the templates below and keep their file names, because I use the names and headings to find each part of your design.

W

Game design document

GameDesign.docx

A 2–4 page description of your game, written in your team's own words under the template's ten headings.

  • What the player is trying to do
  • Every control: each key, click, or tap
  • The rules, and how the player wins or loses
  • What one round of play looks like
  • What's in scope, what's a stretch goal, and what you're leaving out
  • Every use of AI, one line each, in section 10
Draft dueMon, Nov 9
Points25
X

Game data workbook

GameData.xlsx

Every number and list your game needs, one row per item, under the headers already in the template.

  • Levels, characters, enemies, and items
  • Exact values, such as speed, health, points, and time limits
  • The words your game uses, and what each one means
  • Every image or sound, with who made it and its license
  • The Changes sheet: each change your team makes, and later your change requests
Draft dueMon, Nov 16
Points20
P

Presentation and recording

GamePitch.pptx

A 5–8 slide presentation that explains your design to someone who hasn't read the document, and a video of one member presenting it.

  • The central idea, on one slide
  • The gameplay loop, as a diagram
  • The key rules
  • A mock-up of one screen, drawn with shapes
  • A 3–5 minute recording of one member presenting the slides, saved as GamePitch.mp4

How it works

How your design becomes a game

Your team designs the game, and I build it. I use an AI coding agent that works inside a framework designed to check its own work. The agent reads exactly what you write, so the clearer your files are, the closer the game is to what you meant.

  1. Your teamForm your team

    Join a team in Blackboard and introduce yourselves in your team discussion.

  2. Your teamPitch and choose

    Pitch ideas in the team discussion, pick one, assign roles, and create the shared folder.

  3. Your teamWrite and fill in

    Write the design document, fill in the workbook, then build the slides.

  4. InstructorBuild a prototype

    I download your folder after Mon, Nov 23 and build a first playable version over Thanksgiving break.

  5. Your teamPlay and request changes

    Play the prototype and list what should change in the Changes sheet.

  6. InstructorGive feedback

    I reply to your requests as they come in, until Thu, Dec 10.

  7. Your teamSubmit

    Make final edits and submit the folder link in Blackboard by Thu, Dec 10.

  8. InstructorFinal pass and gallery

    After Thu, Dec 10, I make a final pass on your game. If everyone on your team agrees, I post it to the class gallery.

Microsoft 365

Set up your shared folder

Your team creates its shared folder at checkpoint 2, after you choose your game. Until then, use your team discussion in Blackboard.

Use your CCCC Microsoft 365 account for this project. You can work in Word, Excel, and PowerPoint in a web browser, or in the desktop apps with AutoSave turned on. Either way, your teammates see your changes as you make them.

Microsoft 365

  1. Sign in to Microsoft 365.

    Go to microsoft365.com and sign in with your CCCC student email address and password. Then open OneDrive from the app list.

  2. Create the folder (one person).

    Your team's Coordinator selects Create or upload > Folder and names it CIS110 Team 04 – Game Name, using your team number and game name.

  3. Add the templates.

    Download the three templates from this page or from Blackboard, and upload them to the folder. Don't rename them.

  4. Share the folder.

    Select the folder, and then select Share. Enter each teammate's CCCC email address and mine, sgils676@cccc.edu. Set the permission to Can edit, and then select Send.

  5. Add a shortcut (everyone else).

    In OneDrive, open Shared, find your team's folder, and select Add shortcut to My files. The folder now appears with your own files.

  6. Work in the files together.

    Edit the parts you own. To ask a teammate a question, add a comment and type @ followed by their name.

  7. Check your version history.

    To see who changed what, select File > Info > Version History. I use version history, comments, and the Team sheet to see each person's contribution.

  8. Submit the folder link.

    Select the folder, select Share > Copy link, and paste the link into the Blackboard submission.

Google Drive (backup option)

Use Google Drive only if you can't share the folder in Microsoft 365 after you try again in a browser. If you switch, send me a Blackboard message so that I know where to find your files.

  1. Create a folder in Google Drive.

    One person goes to drive.google.com, creates a folder, and names it the same way: CIS110 Team 04 – Game Name.

  2. Upload the templates as they are.

    Upload the three templates without converting them. Google Drive opens Word, Excel, and PowerPoint files in Office editing mode, so they keep their original format.

  3. Share the folder.

    Select Share, add each teammate and me as Editor, and then select Send.

  4. Submit Office files.

    If a file was converted to Google Docs, Sheets, or Slides, select File > Download and choose the Microsoft Office format. Put that copy in the folder before you submit the link.

Presentation recording

Record your presentation

By Monday, November 23, one team member records a video presenting your slides, 3 to 5 minutes long. Talk through each slide as if you were presenting to the class, so that someone who hasn't read your files understands your design.

Your voice is required; your face on camera is optional. Save the video in your shared folder as GamePitch.mp4. It counts toward the presentation's 20 points. I don't post the recording or put it in your game.

PowerPoint desktop app

  1. Open the slides in the desktop app.

    Open GamePitch.pptx from your shared folder. In a browser, select Editing > Open in Desktop App, because PowerPoint for the web can't record narration.

  2. Record the slide show.

    On the Record tab, select From Beginning. On a Mac, select Slide Show > Record Slide Show. Talk through each slide, and press Esc when you finish.

  3. Check it.

    Play the slide show from the beginning and listen. To redo one slide, go to it and select Record > From Current Slide.

  4. Export the video.

    Select File > Export > Create a Video, keep Use Recorded Timings and Narrations, and select Create Video. On a Mac, select File > Export and choose MP4.

  5. Save it in the folder.

    Name the video GamePitch.mp4 and save or upload it to your shared folder.

Without the desktop app

Use this if you can't install or open the PowerPoint desktop app, for example on a school laptop.

  1. Start a screen recording.

    Use any screen recorder that records your voice, such as Clipchamp in a browser or the Snipping Tool on Windows. You can also film the screen with a phone.

  2. Present the slides.

    Open the slides in PowerPoint for the web, select Slide Show, and talk through each slide.

  3. Save it in the folder.

    Save the video as GamePitch.mp4 and upload it to your shared folder. If your recorder saves another format, upload that file and send me a Blackboard message.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to know how to code?

No. You describe the game in Word, Excel, and PowerPoint, and I do the building.

How big should our game be?

Aim for a game that someone can learn in a minute and finish in about five. One screen and one clear goal is a good place to start.

Does it have to be a game?

No. Your team can design a tool instead, such as one for a hobby, a job, or another class. Use the tool version of this page, which has tool versions of the three templates and the same dates and grading.

How does my team talk before we have a shared folder?

Use your team discussion in Blackboard. To reach your team another way, open Groups and send your team a message. I can read every team discussion.

What goes in the Changes sheet?

Each change a teammate needs to know about, such as a new rule or a changed number, with where and why. After the prototype, your change requests go there too. The Changes sheet guide has examples.

What if a teammate stops responding?

Send me a Blackboard message before the next checkpoint. I can see each person's contributions in version history, and I'll follow up with your team.

Can we use the desktop apps instead of the browser?

Yes. Open the files from the shared folder and turn on AutoSave so that your teammates see your changes. Don't work on a copy saved to your computer.

What if our game doesn't get built?

Your grade stays the same. It comes from your three files and your collaboration.

I can't share the folder in Microsoft 365. What should I do?

Sign out, sign back in to microsoft365.com with your CCCC account, and try again in a browser. If it still doesn't work, use the Google Drive backup option in the setup steps and send me a Blackboard message.

Where do I get help?

Send me a Blackboard course message; I reply within 48 business hours. You can also meet with me during student hours on Thursdays from 5:00 PM to 6:00 PM on Zoom. Email sgils676@cccc.edu for the link.
